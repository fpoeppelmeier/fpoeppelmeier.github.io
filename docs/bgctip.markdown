<p class="lead">
BGCTip is an ERC Starting Grant project that investigates how abrupt shifts in the climate system, such as a collapse of the Atlantic overturning circulation, affect the ocean's biological carbon pump and its capacity to take up CO<sub>2</sub>. We use Earth's past abrupt climate events as natural experiments to better constrain what tipping points could mean for the future carbon cycle.
</p>

## Why it matters

The ocean is the largest carbon reservoir that readily exchanges carbon with the atmosphere. A key part of this is the *biological pump*: plankton at the sea surface turn dissolved carbon and nutrients into organic matter, part of which sinks and is stored in the deep ocean. Without it, atmospheric CO<sub>2</sub> would be roughly 50% higher at equilibrium. Yet how the biological pump will respond to ongoing warming remains highly uncertain.

At the same time, continued climate change may push parts of the Earth system across **tipping points**, for example the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) or the large ice sheets. Crossing one tipping point can cascade into others, including the marine carbon cycle. How large these knock-on effects could be is largely unknown, which adds unquantified uncertainty to climate projections.

## Learning from the past

During the last glacial cycle (the past ~120,000 years), Earth's climate repeatedly underwent abrupt changes, the Dansgaard-Oeschger and Heinrich events. They involved rapid weakening and recovery of the AMOC within decades, with impacts that lasted for millennia. These events are natural experiments that let us study how marine biogeochemistry responds to abrupt circulation changes.

Proxy records from marine sediments, ice cores and speleothems document these events. However, each record is local, and its signal is affected by several processes at once. BGCTip therefore combines proxy reconstructions with an Earth system model that **explicitly simulates the proxies themselves**. Model and data can then be compared directly and consistently.

## Approach

BGCTip builds on the [Bern3D Earth system model of intermediate complexity](https://www.climate.unibe.ch). It is efficient enough to simulate multi-millennial climate transitions with a fully coupled ocean, atmosphere, ice sheets and biogeochemistry. ## Collaborations

BGCTip works closely with Martin Frank (GEOMAR, Kiel), Samuel Jaccard (University of Lausanne) and Jörg Lippold (Heidelberg University), who contribute expertise and new proxy reconstructions. The project is linked to the Tipping Point Model Intercomparison Project (TIPMIP), the Horizon Europe projects [ClimTip](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101137601) and [Past to Future](https://past2future.org), and the PAGES working group [IMPACT](https://pastglobalchanges.org/science/current-wg/impact).

## Open science

All model developments and proxy data compilations created in BGCTip will be made openly available, so that the observational and modelling communities can use them. Project publications will be listed here and on the [publications]({{ "/publications/" | relative_url }}) page as they appear.
